Guide

How to run a review session, and what each role sees.

{% if "what_it_does" in visible_sections %}

What Review Robin Web does

Review Robin Web runs structured review sessions: reviewers fill in a form about a group of reviewees, and the app collects, organises, and shares the results. As the operator you decide who reviews whom, what questions they answer, and who sees the outcome.

TermMeaning
SessionOne review round — its own people, form, dates, and results.
OperatorYou: the person who sets up and runs the session.
ReviewerSomeone who fills in the form.
RevieweeSomeone who is being reviewed.
InstrumentA set of questions reviewers answer. A session can hold several.
AssignmentOne reviewer-reviews-one-reviewee pairing.
Observer (optional)Someone who may view collated results without reviewing.

Before you start

  • Review Robin Web is a hosted web app — there is nothing to install. Reach it at the address your administrator gave you.
  • Sign in with your institutional account. The app uses single sign-on, so there is no separate password to create.
  • If your address is on the operator allowlist you land on the Sessions lobby. An empty lobby is expected — you have not made a session yet.
{% endif %} {% if "create_and_set_up" in visible_sections %}

Create and set up a session

On the Sessions lobby, choose Add new, give the session a name and a short unique code, optionally set a deadline and description, and save. New sessions start in Draft: nothing is live, and everything is freely editable.

The Workflow card at the top of every session page is the control panel — it always shows the most useful next action for the session's current state.

Reviewers and reviewees

Use CSV uploads for anything beyond a handful of people. Each roster CSV needs a name column and an email column, plus three tag columns. Use the tags for attributes that drive rule-based assignment — a tutor, a tutorial group — so that, say, each student reviews only their own group.

Headers are ReviewerName, ReviewerEmail, ReviewerTag1–3 for reviewers, and RevieweeName, RevieweeEmail, RevieweeTag1–3 for reviewees. Friendly tag labels can be set later on the Reviewers / Reviewees pages, or keyed into the header after a period — ReviewerTag1.Tutor, with the values still in the column. Leaving a tag header bare on a later re-upload clears the label.

{# 19E rung 4 — the starter templates. Placed here rather than at the top of the card because this is the paragraph that explains the columns they contain: the download is the worked example of the sentence above it. #}

Download setup templates — a zip of reviewers.csv, reviewees.csv, relationships.csv and observers.csv, each with the correct headers and one example row to replace. Fill them in before you create the session and upload them with Quick Setup as you go.

They carry one worked scenario: two students in tutorial group TW01 reviewing each other, their tutor as observer. The tag columns show the naming grammar in use — ReviewerTag1.Tutor and ReviewerTag2.Group on the rosters, PairContextTag1.Interest Group on relationships — with the third tag left bare, since labelling is per column and optional.

Because those labels are in the header, uploading a template unedited renames that session's tag columns to Tutor and Group. Change them to your own vocabulary along with the rows. The same rule works in reverse, so if a session already has the names you want, take its roster from its own Setup page rather than starting from this template — a bare tag header on re-upload clears the name.

Email addresses must be the ones people sign in with. The app matches each person to their roster row by their signed-in address, so a mismatch means they never see their review. This is the single most important setup step — and it is the app's identifier for a person, not an address the app sends mail to.

The Quick Setup card on Session Home takes the roster CSVs (and Relationships and Settings, if you use them) in one place. Working page by page instead, each Setup page has its own upload card. Only a few people? Type them straight in. Either way reviewers and reviewees may overlap, and any row can be marked inactive to exclude someone without deleting them.

Build the form (Instruments)

Each session starts with one instrument you can rename and shape. An instrument carries an assignment rule, response fields (the questions reviewers answer) and display fields (read-only context shown to the reviewer, such as the reviewee's name).

Who reviews whom

Each instrument carries its own assignment rule, set on the Instrument assignment rule band of its card: who does the review, who is being reviewed, and whether the unit of review is each reviewee individually or a group. Setting all three to all, all, individual pairs everyone eligible with everyone else. To narrow it, switch a link to filter and choose the tags and connectors.

Pairs are not generated here — that happens when you Prepare the session. Afterwards the Assignments page shows the resulting pairs per instrument, where you can spot-check coverage and use the Self-reviews toggle.

Optional: visibility, relationships, observers

Per instrument you can set who sees responses and in what form — raw, anonymised, or summarised — for reviewers, reviewees and observers. Turn on Relationships if your review needs per-pairing context, and Observers if some people should view results without reviewing. Both are off by default.

Prepare and launch

  1. On Session Home, use the Workflow card's Prepare session. This generates the assignment pairs from every instrument's rule and runs a pre-flight check. Nothing goes live yet.
  2. Fix anything blocking; warnings are fine to proceed past. The session moves to Validated.
  3. When you are ready, Activate. Instruments open for responses.

Before launching, the Previews page shows the exact form a specific reviewer will see — a good final sanity check.

{% endif %} {% if "give_access" in visible_sections %}

Give reviewers access

The app does not send invitation emails yet, and magic links are not switched on — and you need neither to run a review. Access is based on the roster, so you share the app's address yourself and people sign in.

  1. Make sure the session is Activated and your reviewers are on the roster with the addresses they will sign in with.
  2. Broadcast the app's address through your own channel — an LMS announcement, an email to the class, a course page.
  3. Each person signs in and immediately sees the reviews that apply to them. No invitation or code needed.

Share the link, they sign in, they see their reviews. This is separate from the addresses that identify people on the roster, which are essential.

{% endif %} {% if "watch_progress" in visible_sections %}

Watch progress

The Invitations page shows, per reviewer, how far through the form each person is. Its invitation and reminder columns sit idle until email sending is switched on. The Responses page flips the view: per reviewee, how much coverage they have received.

{% endif %} {% if "close_and_share" in visible_sections %}

Close, release, and share results

  1. Use the Workflow card to Close the session, which stops new responses, and then Release responses when you are ready.
  2. Reviewees see their results on their own page, in the form you allowed.
  3. Observers, if enabled, see collated results on their collation page.

The Extract data page downloads CSVs of everything — reviewers, reviewees, relationships, settings, and the full responses table — for analysis or archiving.

{% endif %} {% if "tips" in visible_sections %}

Tips and troubleshooting

  • Import with CSVs. Beyond a handful of people, upload rather than type — and it is what makes Quick Setup worthwhile.
  • Roster addresses must match sign-in addresses. People are recognised by the address they log in with.
  • No invitation emails needed to launch. Share the app link and signed-in participants see their own reviews.
  • Everything is editable in Draft. Set up freely before validating.
  • Validate early. The pre-flight catches missing pieces before you share the link.
  • Made a mistake after activating? The Workflow card's Revert action returns a session to Draft.
  • Inactive versus delete. Marking someone inactive excludes them while keeping their history; deleting removes them entirely.
  • Reruns. Duplicate a session, or rebuild one from its exported CSVs with Rehydrate from the lobby.
{% endif %} {% if "for_reviewers" in visible_sections %}

For reviewers

  1. Open the link your operator shared and sign in with your institutional account.
  2. Your dashboard lists the reviews that apply to you. Click into one to see the reviewees assigned to you and the questions to answer.
  3. Save as you go and Submit when finished. You can revisit until the session closes.
{% endif %} {% if "for_observers" in visible_sections %}

For observers

An observer views collated results for a session without reviewing anyone. Once the operator releases responses, your collation page shows the results in the form the operator allowed — raw, anonymised, or summarised.

{% endif %} {% if "for_reviewees" in visible_sections %}

For reviewees

A reviewee is someone being reviewed. After the operator closes the session and releases responses, your results appear on your own page, in the form the operator allowed — raw, anonymised, or summarised. Until then there is nothing to see.

{% endif %} {% if "getting_help" in visible_sections %}

Getting help

A session's Validate page explains most setup problems in plain language. For anything else, contact your Review Robin Web administrator.
