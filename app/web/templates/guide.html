Guide

How to run a review session, and what each role sees.

What Review Robin Web does

Review Robin Web runs structured review sessions: reviewers fill in a form about a group of reviewees, and the app collects, organises, and shares the results. As the operator you decide who reviews whom, what questions they answer, and who sees the outcome.

TermMeaning
SessionOne review round — its own people, form, dates, and results.
OperatorYou: the person who sets up and runs the session.
ReviewerSomeone who fills in the form.
RevieweeSomeone who is being reviewed.
InstrumentA set of questions reviewers answer. A session can hold several.
AssignmentOne reviewer-reviews-one-reviewee pairing.
Observer (optional)Someone who may view collated results without reviewing.

Create and set up a session

Create a session

On the Sessions lobby, choose Add new session.

Give the session a name and a short unique code, optionally set a deadline and description.

Indicate whether you will need the Relationships and Observers tabs and pages. Note that sessions do not need these to run (optional).

If you already have the CSV files for the reviewers and reviewees (or Relationships, Observers, and Session settings), you may upload them in advance using Quick setup.

Click on Create Session to create the session.

Finding your way around

Use the tabs at the top of the session pages to navigate to different pages to fully set up the session, or to operate the review. The status bar below the tabs provides a summary of the session's status.

In the screen cap below, the status bar shows that this session is in Draft mode, that 154 Reviewers and 154 Reviewees have been added to the session, and that there is 1 instrument, but it's not set up yet, hence 0 / 1. The Workflow card provides guidance on what needs to be done next — here, Reviewers and Reviewees have been populated, but the review's Instruments are not ready.

Reviewers and reviewees

Every session must have at least one reviewer and at least one reviewee; relationships and observers are optional. You can populate a session using CSV uploads or add them individually on the relevant pages. Use CSV uploads for anything beyond a handful of people.

CSV rosters can be uploaded via the Quick Setup card (when creating the session or on the Session home page) or via the individual upload cards on each of the Reviewers, Reviewees, Relationships, and Observers pages. The files should be in UTF-8 with max 5000 rows.

The required and optional columns are listed in each Upload card. A full set of template CSV files with mock data can be downloaded here — reviewers.csv, reviewees.csv, relationships.csv and observers.csv, each with the correct headers and example rows to replace.

Apart from the CSV upload, you can also add or edit the friendly tag labels through the Reviewers, Reviewees and Relationships pages:

Note that uploading a template with a bare tag header will clear the existing friendly tag label. The labels travel in the CSV header after a period — ReviewerTag1.Tutor, with the values still in the column — so the header is the one place a label is carried, in both directions.

The app uses email addresses to identify persons in the session. This means that each person (reviewer, reviewee, observer) should be uniquely associated with one email address. The app will fail to validate a session if the same email address is associated with more than one name. The converse, however, is fine, since two different persons (identified by distinct email addresses) may have the same name.

For review participants who need to access the app, their email addresses must be the ones tied to their institutional MS365 accounts. That is typically reviewers and observers — but reviewees too, if you need them to see responses. The app matches each person to their roster row by their signed-in address, so a mismatch means they never see their review.

Build the form (Instruments)

Each session starts with one instrument you can rename and shape. An instrument carries an assignment rule, response fields (the questions reviewers answer) and display fields (read-only context shown to the reviewer, such as the reviewee's name). Click the chevron on the instrument's bar to expand or collapse the whole instrument card.

The expanded card has three main parts. Unlock the card to make any edits, and don't forget to save. The first part lets you rename the instrument and set its assignment rule:

Each instrument carries its own assignment rule, set on the Instrument assignment rule band: who does the review, who is being reviewed, and whether the unit of review is each reviewee individually or a group. Setting all three to all, all, individual pairs everyone eligible with everyone else. To narrow it, switch a link to filter and choose the tags and connectors by clicking the pills. In the screencap above the instrument has a rule where all reviewers are given this form, each reviewing the people who share the same Tag 1 as them (here given the friendly label Group), and they review each subject individually rather than as a group.

Pairs are not generated here — that happens when you Prepare the session.

The second part is a preview of what the reviewer will see. Use the text boxes to fill in a description of the instrument and helping text for the response fields. The display and response fields can be deselected and selected using the pills at the bottom, and reordered by dragging and dropping; column widths can be adjusted by dragging the edges. After changing the assignment rule, use Refresh sample to refresh the preview.

The last part lets you add, delete and edit response fields. Use the R button to make a field required, and the ≡ button to call up a help-text box for that field. Don't forget to save any edits for them to take effect.

Per instrument you can set who sees responses and in what form — raw, Anonymized, or Summarized — for reviewers, reviewees and observers.

At the bottom of the card, Replicate copies the current instrument as a new one below it, while +Instrument creates a new one with the default Rating and Comment fields. +Page break inserts a page break in the reviewer's form.

An instrument counts as set up once it has at least one visible response field and all three assignment links have been set — which is what the status bar's Instruments pill counts.

Optional: relationships and observers

Turn on Relationships if your review needs per-pairing context, and Observers if some people should view results without reviewing. Both are off by default.

Prepare and activate

Generate the assignment pairs using the Prepare session button in the Workflow card:

If validation passed, the Workflow card will allow you to create the invites and activate the session. Warnings and blocking issues will be shown on the right side of the Workflow card; more details can also be found on the Validate page.

You can use the Assignments page to inspect the assignment pairs, or to deactivate self-review assignments (i.e., assignment pairs where the reviewer is the same as the reviewee). Note that doing so will not delete those assignment pairs but only make them inactive.

You can use the Previews page to check what a selected reviewer will see on their side. Note that the preview is generated using the same underlying engine that creates the form for that reviewer.

Activate the session when you are satisfied that everything is ok using the Workflow card. At that point, the Workflow card will give you the option to revert the session to draft mode, or to close the session manually (when the responses have been collected).

If you set a Start and End date/time when setting up the session, the app will prepare and activate the session at Start and close the session at End.

{% endif %} {% if "give_access" in visible_sections %}

Give reviewers access

As the email function is not implemented yet, you can notify the reviewers by sharing the app's address. Reviewers will use their MS365 account to sign in to see the reviews that apply to them — no invitation or code needed.

  1. Make sure the session is Activated and your reviewers are on the roster with the institutional MS365 addresses they will sign in with.
  2. Broadcast the app's address through your own channel — an LMS announcement, an email to the class, a course page.
  3. Each person signs in and immediately sees the reviews that apply to them.
{% endif %} {% if "watch_progress" in visible_sections %}

Watch progress

The Invitations page monitors the reviewers, showing who has started or completed their reviews. (Note that the reminder function, being dependent upon email, is also currently not implemented yet.)

The Responses page flips the view to the reviewees, to reflect how many responses each has received.

{% endif %} {% if "close_and_share" in visible_sections %}

Download responses

At the close of the session, download the collected data using the Extract data page. You can also use the same page to extract a complete set of CSV files that sets up the session.

Before that, use the Workflow card to Close the session, which stops new responses, and then Release responses when you are ready. Reviewees then see their results on their own page, in the form you allowed, and observers, if enabled, see collated results on their collation page.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • Import with CSVs. Beyond a handful of people, upload rather than type — and it is what makes Quick Setup worthwhile.
  • Roster addresses must match sign-in addresses. People are recognized by the institutional MS365 address they log in with.
  • No invitation emails needed to launch. Share the app link and signed-in participants see their own reviews.
  • Follow the instructions on the Workflow card shown on the Session home page and all the operations pages if you are unsure what to do next.
  • Everything is editable in Draft. Set up freely before validating.
  • Validate early. The pre-flight catches missing pieces before you share the link.
  • Made a mistake after activating? The Workflow card's Revert action returns a session to Draft.
  • Inactive versus delete. Marking someone inactive excludes them while keeping their history; deleting removes them entirely.
  • Reruns. Duplicate a session, or rebuild one from its exported CSVs with Rehydrate from the lobby.
  • The Validate page explains most setup problems in plain language. For anything else, contact your Review Robin Web administrator.

Sample session

If you would rather see a working session than read about one, this builds you a populated one in a couple of minutes — the whole flow, with nobody real involved.

Download sample session data — the same four files as the setup templates, but filled in: six students across two tutorial groups TW01 and TW02, each reviewing the others in their group, with both tutors as observers.

  1. Create a new session and give it a name you will recognise as disposable.
  2. In Quick Setup, attach all four files and submit.
  3. Prepare session from the Workflow card. The session reaches Validated with assignments generated — enough of them that the Assignments page, the Responses grid and observer collation all have something to show.
  4. Activate it and look around. Every page is populated, so you can see what your reviewers, reviewees and observers will see.

Everyone in it is fictional and every address is @example.edu, so no mail could reach a real person even once sending is switched on. Archive or delete the session when you are done with it. The tag columns carry the same Tutor and Group names as the setup templates, so uploading these files renames that session's tag columns to match.

For reviewers

Your dashboard lists the reviews that apply to you. Click into one to see the reviewees assigned to you and the questions to answer. Save as you go and Submit when finished. You can revisit until the session closes.

{% endif %} {% if "for_observers" in visible_sections %}

For observers

An observer views collated results for a session without reviewing anyone. Once the operator releases responses, your collation page shows the results in the form the operator allowed — raw, anonymised, or summarised.

{% endif %} {% if "for_reviewees" in visible_sections %}

For reviewees

A reviewee is someone being reviewed. After the operator closes the session and releases responses, your results appear on your own page, in the form the operator allowed — raw, anonymised, or summarised. Until then there is nothing to see.
